# WKNDR naming research, October 2026

_Research memo, written 5 October 2026 from the public trade mark registers, domain registries and social platforms as they stood that day. This is preliminary research by a non-lawyer and it is not legal advice. Section 1.4 lists what a trade mark attorney would need to confirm before any filing or rename decision._

## The answer in short

Nobody holds a live registration for WKNDR itself in the classes that matter, in any of the four registers searched (EUIPO, BOIP, UKIPO, USPTO). The worry that WKNDR "may be hard to trademark" is nonetheless well founded, for two reasons that are different from the one the worry usually assumes.

The first reason is crowding. WKND, one letter away, is registered in all four registers by three different owners: for event discovery software in the EU, the UK and the US, for weekend trip planning software in the US, and for weekend publishing in the Benelux. One of those owners has already opposed a third party's WKND filing at the EUIPO, and that filing was withdrawn. The full word WEEKENDER is registered for entertainment and travel services in the EU, the UK and the US. Any WKNDR filing for this product would have to get past those marks, and any use of WKNDR has to live next to them.

The second reason is weakness. Examiners read WKNDR as "weekender". The USPTO has said so in writing four times since 2014: twice refusing WEEKNDR (2014 and 2017) as merely describing the intended user of weekend travel services, and twice refusing WKNDR (2024 and 2026) as the phonetic equivalent of WEEKENDER. The EUIPO and UKIPO guidelines apply the same rule to text speak and dropped vowels. A weekend planning app called "weekender" is close to the descriptive line, so even a registration would be narrow and hard to enforce.

The practical consequence is that WKNDR is fine for what it is today, a small test app used by its makers and friends, and poor as a house brand to carry across cities and to spend brand money on. Section 2 gives the decision rule. Section 4 gives eight alternatives that were screened the same way, and section 5 recommends one.

## 1. WKNDR itself

### 1.1 Freedom to use

The question here is whether someone else holds an identical or near identical mark in the relevant classes, so that using WKNDR could infringe it. The relevant classes are 9 (software and apps), 35 (advertising and promotion), 41 (entertainment and event information), 42 (software as a service) and 39 (travel).

Method. Each register was queried for WKNDR and its close variants (WKND, WKNDRS, WKNDER, WEEKNDR, WEEKENDR, WEEKENDER, WEEKND) on 5 October 2026. TMview, the EUIPO's cross-register search, was used for the comparative sweep across EUIPO, BOIP, UKIPO, USPTO and WIPO international registrations. Hits that matter were then verified on the register's own site: EUIPO eSearch plus (which also confirmed one WKNDR search result, the Korean apparel mark), the UKIPO case pages, and USPTO TSDR including the full file histories. Two caveats are stated plainly. BOIP's own register sat behind an automated bot check that I did not work around, so the two Benelux records are taken from TMview, to which BOIP supplies its data. UKIPO's text search page raised the same kind of check after a few page views, so UK list results are from TMview and individual UK records were verified on the UKIPO case pages. TMview states that it is not an official register.

### 1.2 Ability to protect

The question here is whether WKNDR, a vowel dropped "weekender", would be treated as descriptive or weak for weekend planning software and event information. The short answer is that all four offices read a common misspelling or text speak form as the word it stands for, and then ask whether that word describes the goods or services. "Weekender" means a person who goes away or goes out for the weekend. For a product whose job is planning weekends, that is the intended user and the purpose, which is the territory where descriptiveness refusals happen. The evidence below is mixed in the way one would expect: some examiners have let WKND through, and others have refused WEEKNDR.

#### How the offices treat dropped vowels

United States. TMEP section 1209.03(j) says a novel spelling that is the phonetic equivalent of a merely descriptive word is also merely descriptive if purchasers would perceive it as that word. The USPTO applied exactly this to WEEKNDR in [September 2014](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/documentviewer?caseId=sn86284679&docId=OOA20140908131204) and again in [March 2017](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/documentviewer?caseId=sn87254905&docId=OOA20170314114217), both times for weekend travel booking services. The 2014 action calls the mark a novel spelling of "weekender" that describes the intended user of the services; the 2017 action says the same and adds a likelihood of confusion refusal against Travelzoo's WEEKENDS.COM. Both applicants gave up.

For WKNDR specifically, the USPTO has twice refused registration on the ground that the mark is the phonetic equivalent of WEEKENDER: a [non-final action in April 2024](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/documentviewer?caseId=sn97743032&docId=NFIN20240423112658) against five WEEKENDER clothing registrations and a hotel registration, and a [final refusal in February 2026](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/documentviewer?caseId=sn99219388&docId=FREF20260221093925), which notes that there is no correct pronunciation of a mark and that the compared marks "could clearly be pronounced the same". Neither of those cases raised descriptiveness, because the goods were t-shirts, travel blogs and vacation rentals rather than weekend planning. The lesson is the equivalence itself: for the USPTO, WKNDR is WEEKENDER.

The other side of the US picture is that WKND has been registered on the Principal Register, without any disclaimer or claim of acquired distinctiveness, for "software for planning weekend travel trips" ([Reg. 6267728](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=88651688&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch), examined in January 2020 with only an [examiner's amendment](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/documentviewer?caseId=sn88651688&docId=EXA20200121094508) to the wording) and for event search software ([Reg. 5564287](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=87732710&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch), where the only [office action](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/documentviewer?caseId=sn87732710&docId=OOA20180409201455) concerned the wording of the goods). Two other WKND formatives in events and hotels went to the Supplemental Register, the register for marks that are descriptive but capable of becoming distinctive. The honest reading is that US examiners are inconsistent on "WKND" for weekend services, and that "weekender" for weekend services has been treated as descriptive whenever the examiner looked at the meaning.

European Union. The EUIPO guidelines (Part B, Section 4, Chapter 4, "Misspellings and omissions") state that a misspelling does not necessarily change the descriptive character of a sign, that words are often misspelt to look more fashionable, and that XTRA, XPERT, EASI-CASH, LITE, RELY-ABLE and FRESHHH were all refused. A misspelling only helps when it is striking, surprising or changes the meaning (D'LICIOUS and FANTASTICK were accepted; LINQ and LIQID were accepted because the unusual letter changes the look and the sound). The same chapter treats abbreviations of descriptive terms as descriptive when the public recognises them. The text is on the [EUIPO guidelines site](https://guidelines.euipo.europa.eu/) and in the [chapter PDF](https://euipo.europa.eu/tunnel-web/secure/webdav/guest/document_library/contentPdfs/law_and_practice/trade_marks_practice_manual/WP_2_2017/Part-B/04-part_b_examination_section_4_absolute_grounds_for_refusal/part_B_examination_section_4_chapter_4/TC/part_B_examination_section_4_chapter_4_Descriptive_tm_7-1-c_tc_en.pdf) I read. The closest recent example is BETTR, a vowel dropped "better" filed for software and services in classes 9, 35, 36, 38 and 42: refused in April 2025 and, after an appeal (decision [R 970/2025-1](https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearchCLW/#key/trademark/APL_20250825_R0970_2025-1_019124972) of 25 August 2025) that annulled only part of the refusal, the application stands [refused](https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/#details/trademarks/019124972). The Board reasoned that native speakers autocorrect BETTR to "better".

Against that, the EUIPO has registered WKNDTRP as a word mark for travel planning apps, and WKND MGMT for event software, and the full word "Weekender" for travel arrangement and club nights. None of those files shows an absolute grounds objection. An examiner looking at WKNDR for "software for planning weekend activities" could go either way; the safer assumption is a weak mark.

United Kingdom. The UKIPO [examination guide](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/trade-marks-manual/the-examination-guide), under "Phonetic equivalents of objectionable words (misspellings)", says common misspellings and text speak are objectionable if the conventionally spelled word is descriptive or non distinctive, and gives LRN 2 TXT for training services as an example. Uncommon misspellings that change the meaning or need mental effort may be acceptable. "WKNDR" is text speak of the common kind. The UK register also shows how the office treats "weekender": the word has been accepted inside about two dozen festival marks, which only happens because it is a shared descriptive word that nobody can own alone.

Benelux. BOIP applies the same test under the Benelux Convention on Intellectual Property (article 2.2bis) and is known for examining descriptiveness strictly. I could not read BOIP's own guideline PDF because the site's bot check blocked it. The register itself shows that BOIP registered the plain word WEEKENDER in 2016 for artist coaching and exhibitions (now expired) and WKND as a logo for Mediahuis in 2025. Logos carry their own distinctiveness, so the Mediahuis registration says little about the word.

#### What this means for WKNDR

As a mark for this product, WKNDR will be read as "weekender" by every office. For event information and weekend planning that is a description of the user and the purpose, so a refusal for descriptiveness is possible in the US, the UK and the Benelux and cannot be ruled out at the EUIPO. If a registration is obtained, its scope would be the odd spelling rather than the word, and it would not stop a competitor calling itself "Weekender Rotterdam". Earlier WKND marks in exactly this lane make clearance harder than the descriptiveness question does. Taken together, WKNDR is a name that can be used quietly but cannot be owned well.

### 1.4 What a trade mark attorney would need to confirm

This memo is a knock out search by a non-lawyer, on one day, using the public interfaces of the registers. It is not a clearance opinion and it is not legal advice. Before filing or before deciding the rename on the basis of this memo, an attorney would need to confirm the following.

A full clearance search including figurative marks, phonetic and visual variants (WEEKEND plus suffix, WEEKENDS, WKEND, WEKEND, and the weekday abbreviations), and in the Benelux the trade name register (KvK) and company names, because Dutch trade name law protects unregistered names used in trade.

## 2. A decision rule

Keep WKNDR through the city tests if all of the following stay true: no live identical mark in classes 9, 41 or 42 in the Benelux or the EU (true today); the app stays free, small and used by people you know; no money is spent on brand assets, paid acquisition or printed material under the name; and nobody has written to you. In that state the name harms no one and the exposure is a rename later, which is cheap while the audience is small.

Rename before spending on brand if any of these becomes true: you plan paid acquisition, press, partnerships with publishers, or any spend you would be sad to lose; you want to file a trade mark (the filing itself invites the WKND owners to look); a second city outside the Netherlands is scheduled, and especially a US city, where WKND is registered for weekend planning and event search and a WKNDR tour company already trades; or an attorney's clearance opinion rates the confusion risk against the WKND marks as anything above low. New Orleans fails this test on its own.

Rename now if: you receive a letter from any WKND or WEEKENDER owner; a WKNDR or WKND application in classes 9, 41 or 42 is filed in the Benelux or the EU by someone else (watch the pending UK SOCA WKNDR and US WEEKENDR filings as the early signs of a trend); you raise money or sign anything where the brand is part of the value; or you decide the house brand has to be the same word in every city, because that word cannot be WKNDR in the US.

By this rule, as of October 2026: keep WKNDR for the Amsterdam and Rotterdam tests, do not file for it, do not spend on it, and choose the successor now so the second city launches under the name that will last.

## 5. Recommendation

Keep WKNDR for the Amsterdam and Rotterdam tests, file nothing under it, spend nothing on it, and plan the rename for the first city outside the Netherlands, because that is the first moment the name has to stand on its own and the US is where WKND is registered for exactly this job and a WKNDR tour company already trades.
